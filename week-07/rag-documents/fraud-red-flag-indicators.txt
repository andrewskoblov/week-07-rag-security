FRAUD AND MONEY LAUNDERING RED FLAG INDICATORS
Financial Institution Compliance Reference Guide
BSA/AML Transaction Monitoring — Red Flag Compendium

=============================================================
SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION TO RED FLAG INDICATORS
=============================================================

Red flag indicators are observable behaviors, transaction patterns, or account characteristics that may suggest a customer is engaged in financial crime, including money laundering, fraud, terrorist financing, or other illicit activity. Red flags do not automatically indicate criminal activity — they are signals that warrant additional scrutiny, enhanced due diligence, or a suspicious activity report filing determination.

Financial institutions are expected to implement transaction monitoring systems and compliance programs capable of detecting red flag activity. Examiners from the OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve, and NCUA assess whether an institution's BSA/AML program is adequately calibrated to detect the red flags relevant to its customer base and risk profile. Failure to detect and respond to red flag activity is a primary finding in enforcement actions against financial institutions.

Red flags are organized below by category. Individual flags carry more weight when they appear in combination with one another. A single red flag may not warrant a SAR filing, but multiple concurrent red flags involving the same customer or transaction series are a strong indicator of suspicious activity requiring investigation.

=============================================================
SECTION 2: STRUCTURING AND CURRENCY-RELATED RED FLAGS
=============================================================

2.1 Structuring Indicators

Structuring — the practice of breaking up transactions to avoid the $10,000 Currency Transaction Report (CTR) threshold — is itself a federal crime under 31 U.S.C. § 5324, regardless of whether the underlying funds are from illegal sources. The following behaviors are indicators of structuring:

- Customer makes multiple cash deposits on the same day at different branches totaling just under $10,000
- Customer makes cash deposits of $9,000, $9,500, or similar amounts just below the CTR threshold on consecutive days
- Multiple individuals make deposits to the same account on the same day, each below $10,000 (smurfing)
- Customer asks teller how much cash they can deposit without triggering paperwork or reporting
- Customer becomes nervous, agitated, or leaves the bank when informed that a CTR must be filed
- Customer breaks a single large transaction into smaller ones after being told about reporting requirements
- Account receives numerous small deposits from multiple parties followed by rapid consolidation and withdrawal
- Round-number transactions just below reporting thresholds (e.g., exactly $9,999 or $4,999)

2.2 Unusual Currency Activity

- Business account shows cash deposits inconsistent with the nature of the business (e.g., a software company making large cash deposits)
- Customer exchanges large amounts of small-denomination bills for large-denomination bills without a plausible business reason
- Customer deposits cash that is bundled in rubber bands, wrapped in plastic, or has an unusual odor
- Cash deposits made by third parties unrelated to the account holder with no apparent business relationship
- Significant increase in cash activity with no corresponding change in business activity or revenue explanation

SECTION 3: ACCOUNT BEHAVIOR RED FLAGS
=============================================================

3.1 Account Opening Red Flags

- Customer is reluctant to provide complete identification information or provide information required by law
- Customer provides identification documents that appear altered, inconsistent, or do not match other information provided
- Customer opens multiple accounts in a short period without a clear business reason
- Customer opens account specifically to receive a single wire transfer and then closes it
- Business customer cannot clearly explain the nature of its business or expected transaction activity
- Customer is accompanied by another person who seems to be directing their actions or providing answers on their behalf
- Customer uses different addresses, phone numbers, or identification for different accounts

3.2 Transaction Pattern Red Flags

- Account receives large deposits that are immediately withdrawn or transferred with no intermediate activity
- Account shows a pattern of large cash deposits followed by immediate wire transfers to foreign accounts
- Funds are transferred through a series of accounts with no apparent business purpose (layering)
- Account receives transfers from many unrelated parties and immediately consolidates and moves the funds
- Customer requests wire transfers to countries identified as high-risk for money laundering or terrorist financing
- Transactions occur at unusual hours (late night, early morning) inconsistent with account type
- Account activity significantly deviates from the customer risk profile established at account opening
- Customer frequently requests replacement debit cards or checks without reporting them lost or stolen

3.3 Business Account Red Flags

- Business account used primarily for personal expenses unrelated to the stated business purpose
- Business has no employees but receives payroll-sized deposits
- Business shows revenue inconsistent with its size, type, or location (e.g., a small retail store reporting millions in cash sales)
- Business customer cannot identify its customers, suppliers, or the nature of its transactions
- Business makes payments to shell companies in offshore jurisdictions
- Commingling of personal and business funds in ways that obscure the source of money

SECTION 4: WIRE TRANSFER AND ELECTRONIC PAYMENT RED FLAGS
=============================================================

4.1 Domestic Wire Transfer Red Flags

- Multiple wire transfers sent to the same beneficiary from different originators in a short period
- Wire transfer instructions include unusual payment details such as references to "loans," "gifts," or "commissions" for transactions that do not match the customer's profile
- Customer requests that wire transfer beneficiary information not be included in the transfer
- Wires sent to or received from law firms, real estate escrow accounts, or shell companies without clear business purpose
- Customer sends or receives wires just below internal monitoring thresholds (threshold avoidance)

4.2 International Wire Transfer Red Flags

- Wire transfers to or from high-risk jurisdictions identified on the FATF Non-Cooperative Countries list or subject to U.S. sanctions
- Customer cannot explain the purpose of international wire transfers or the relationship to the foreign beneficiary
- Transactions involve jurisdictions with strong bank secrecy laws and limited AML oversight
- Transfers involve intermediary banks or shell companies in multiple countries with no clear business rationale (classic layering behavior)
- A customer with no history of international business suddenly begins sending or receiving large international wires
- Wire amounts cluster just below the $3,000 threshold requiring collection of beneficiary information under the Travel Rule

SECTION 5: FRAUD-SPECIFIC RED FLAGS
=============================================================

5.1 Identity Theft and Account Takeover

- Customer reports they did not authorize transactions that appear on their account
- Multiple failed login attempts followed by a successful login from an unrecognized device or IP address
- Changes to account contact information (address, phone, email) shortly before a large transaction
- Multiple accounts showing the same contact information or device fingerprint
- New payees added to online banking bill pay followed immediately by large payments
- Customer's personal information matches that of a recently deceased individual
- Social Security numbers associated with multiple different names or dates of birth

5.2 Check Fraud Red Flags

- Checks presented for payment that have inconsistencies in font, paper quality, or MICR line encoding
- Customer deposits a large check and immediately requests cash back or a portion to be wired
- Multiple checks drawn on the same account presented at different branches or locations
- Check amounts are altered — original amounts crossed out and larger amounts substituted
- Business checks presented by individuals who do not appear to be employees or authorized representatives
- High volume of returned items (NSF, account closed, stop payment) on an account

5.3 Mortgage and Real Estate Fraud Red Flags

- Property sold multiple times in a short period with rapidly escalating prices not supported by market conditions (property flipping)
- Loan application contains inconsistencies between stated income, employment, or assets and verifiable records
- Purchase price significantly above or below appraised value without explanation
- Buyer provides large earnest money deposits in cash
- Multiple parties to a real estate transaction share the same address, phone number, or email
- Seller receives proceeds at closing via wire to an account in a different name

5.4 Elder Financial Exploitation Red Flags

- Elderly customer accompanied by a new acquaintance or caregiver who directs transactions or speaks on their behalf
- Sudden changes in account signatories, power of attorney, or beneficiary designations for elderly customers
- Large cash withdrawals or wire transfers by an elderly customer who has no prior history of such transactions
- Customer appears confused, fearful, or is in apparent disagreement with the person accompanying them
- New individual begins making transactions on behalf of an elderly customer without clear legal authorization
- Transactions inconsistent with the customer's known income, assets, or lifestyle (e.g., a fixed-income retiree wiring $50,000 overseas)

SECTION 6: ENHANCED DUE DILIGENCE TRIGGERS
=============================================================

When red flags are identified, institutions must conduct enhanced due diligence (EDD) to determine whether a SAR filing is warranted. EDD steps include:

- Reviewing all transaction history for the customer and linked accounts for the past 12–24 months
- Contacting the customer for a plausible explanation of the flagged activity
- Verifying the customer's identity and beneficial ownership information
- Reviewing publicly available information including news searches, court records, and OFAC sanctions lists
- Consulting prior SAR filings or law enforcement inquiries involving the customer
- Escalating to the SAR Review Committee with full documentation of findings

All EDD steps and findings must be documented in the customer's compliance file regardless of whether a SAR is ultimately filed. Documentation of the investigation process is critical during regulatory examinations and demonstrates that the institution acted in good faith.
